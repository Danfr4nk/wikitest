## The argument

New York took two runs at Dan and both ended the same way: **an exit
forced by a vertical authority, documented to the hilt on the way out.**
NYC-1 (2010–2013) ends with the Creative License whistleblower exit —
four acts, an IRS and NYS Department of Labor complaint, a LinkedIn
intellectual-property retaliation. NYC-2 (2019–2025) ends with the Au
Za'atar shed shutdown — 41 months, his longest job, ruled involuntary.
Between them, the Uniontown years (Nemacolin caddying 2016–2019, the Fran
vigil) read as the intermission the city made necessary.

The two eras are not parallel lives. They are the same shape fourteen
years apart: a trusted vertical figure's undocumented financial claim met
with exit and documentation rather than confrontation — the corpus itself
names Creative License and the 2026 BFS case the first and most recent
instances of that shape ([`dat:0498`](../data/0498-creative-license-nyc-2011-2012.md)).

## The evidence it synthesizes

**NYC-1: arrival.** The day after the 2010-02-17 Suboxone day-zero, Rick
meets Dan in Pittsburgh and they board an Amtrak to Penn Station; months
of apartment-hunting follow on Rick's $2,000/month "while you intern"
offer; lease at 424 Bedford Ave, Williamsburg, by June, with Alexis —
who was present for the whole pre-stabilization arc
([`evt:2010-02-17-suboxone-start`](../events/2010-02-17-suboxone-start.md),
[`ent:alexis-armel`](../entities/alexis-armel.md)). The city's first
employment is studio-adjacent: an internship/engineer role at Ishlab
Studios under Jamin Gilbert, 10 Jay Street, DUMBO — still no page of its
own — then Creative License, a small celebrity-talent and licensed-music
brokering company at 71 8th Avenue, West Village, under president Kevin
McKiernan, ~April 2011 to February 15, 2012, contemporaneous title
"Special Projects Coordinator"
([`dat:0498`](../data/0498-creative-license-nyc-2011-2012.md)).

**NYC-1: the exit, in four acts.** Act Zero — the airfare citation, Dan's
stated reason for quitting, which his own primary testimony settles as
real: *"the thing i cited when i quit had to do with some kind of small
fraud with airplane tickets that i blew it up into a much much bigger
thing but maybe i was just desensitized"* — with explicit retrospective
self-doubt about proportionality. He used it as "'legal ground' to
detach" because a personality complaint would have been dismissed as him
being "sensitive," while a "financial anomaly" made the departure "an
objective failure" on the company's part. Act One — the payroll dispute:
a final paycheck with a $104 MetroCard charge he says he never requested,
Marty Jackson (Accounting) demanding opt-out "in writing before you
quit" and writing the anchor line — *"It's a trust issue. We don't trust
you. Please do not forget the keys or any other CL property you have"* —
Dan filing IRS and NYS Department of Labor complaints, Rick CC'd
throughout functioning as tactical coach (*"You sound so desperate. That
is what kevin wants. We'll talk about a strategy for this"* — the
corpus's clearest instance of Rick operating as strategist rather than
distant authority figure), Dan closing it out directly with Marty on
April 9, conceding the $104 and explicitly separating the accountant
from the president. Act Two — the catharsis: a one-line April 26, 2012
email to Rachel Rauch, "Enjoy!!!", with compiled McKiernan voicemails.
Act Three — the whistleblower disclosure, May 2012, unprompted, to
former contact Renae Holland: in December 2011 McKiernan had used Dan's
own email/LinkedIn account to send recommendation-removal requests to
former employees, and the employment contracts Dan was made to send to
two former interns had been altered before sending, a clause added
prohibiting recommendations without CEO approval. Renae's reply
corroborates from her own tenure: *"in my time there I was Kevin falsify
more documents that I can count."* Act Four — retaliation, June 2012:
McKiernan filed a formal LinkedIn IP complaint backed by two notarized
documents asserting Dan had never been a Creative License employee —
notarized by Katherine Palakovich, who had sent Dan his own non-compete
in May 2011, using her notary commission on her employer's behalf
against a former coworker. LinkedIn threatened termination within three
business days; Dan filed a counter-notice naming the retaliation motive
([`dat:0498`](../data/0498-creative-license-nyc-2011-2012.md)).

The page is the best-documented instance of the vertical-authority
pattern below the 2026 BFS case ([`dat:1387`](../data/1387-vertical-authority-skepticism.md)).

**The intermission.** Nemacolin Woodlands Resort, Farmington PA — golf
caddy, April 2016 – November 2019, a resume-derived 43-month span — the
main income through the hardest financial stretch, overlapping the Fran
vigil, the Suboxone years, and the 2018–2020 Uniontown supply-network
era. Held-corroborated beats (Laurel Valley day trips, the Pikewood
National tournament coordination) sit beside unheld resume narrative
([`ent:nemacolin`](../entities/nemacolin.md)).

**NYC-2: Au Za'atar.** March 2021 – August 2024, 41 months — his longest
job and his most-visited GPS location (445 visits), hired as a busser
with zero food-service experience, ending with the August 2024 shed
shutdown, ruled involuntary (dossier Amendment 2, ACCEPTED). The Midtown
restaurant (58th & 1st); the plywood COVID-era "Shed" on the 58th Street
sidewalk, rebuilt nightly, which Dan turned into the "Outside Guy" post
with sole custody of the shed's cash tips — never closed once in 41
months. Pay confirmed by Dan directly 2026-07-14: $15/hour base plus tip
pool, weekly Sunday checks typically $800–1,000 for a six-day week.
Contemporaneous first-person, held corpus, 2024-08-21: *"By the way they
officially let me go from AZ"*; *"Tarik was really really nice about it
and everything though. We parted on good terms"*
([`ent:au-zaatar`](../entities/au-zaatar.md),
[`evt:2021-03-au-zaatar-hired`](../events/2021-03-au-zaatar-hired.md),
[`evt:2024-08-au-zaatar-exit`](../events/2024-08-au-zaatar-exit.md)).

The tenure's human texture: Tarik Fallous, the owner-operator who hired
Dan on the spot ([`ent:tarik-fallous`](../entities/tarik-fallous.md));

## What it explains that the parts don't

Neither era's nodes carry the shape that only appears when they are laid
side by side: **Dan does not get fired from New York; he documents his
way out of it.** Creative License is fourteen years before BFS Foods,
and the corpus names them the first and most recent instances of one
shape — a trusted vertical figure's undocumented financial claim met
with exit and documentation rather than confrontation. The 2026 BFS
drawer dispute ($50, disputed, termination, rehire offered) is the third
instance. That is not a job history. That is a behavioral signature with
a fourteen-year period.

It also explains the asymmetry between the two exits' paperwork. The
Creative License exit produced IRS complaints, a counter-notice, a
paper trail Dan still cites fourteen years later. The Au Za'atar exit
produced *"Tarik was really really nice about it"* and unemployment
filed August 8. Same city, same structural position (the worker with the
cash tips, the shed's sole custody), opposite paperwork — because the
first vertical authority was an adversary and the second was, by Dan's
own account, the only boss he ever loved. The documentation instinct
fires at betrayal, not at loss.

## Where it breaks and what it can't settle

**The held corpus is nearly silent on the restaurant.** The tenure's
texture rests almost entirely on unheld sources — page testimony, the
dossiers, the old wiki. The Au Za'atar entity is one of the nodes most
dependent on material this repository does not hold. The 41 months, the
$800–1,000 checks, the never-closed shed: all page testimony, none of it
corpus-verified.

**The 2013–2016 window is the biography's least-documented adult
stretch.** The city years end with the LinkedIn retaliation (June 2012)
and the record resumes, in employment terms, with Nemacolin in 2016.
What happened in between is not in this KB.

**The airfare mechanism is still unverified.** Dan's own testimony
settles that the citation is real; the mechanism — what the fraud
actually was — stays open, as does what happened to Renae Holland
afterward ("his attack against me"). The triple-pass correction history
on that page (flagged, wrongly unflagged, re-flagged correctly, three
passes in one day) is itself a case study in the correction instinct
overfiring — see [`dat:0498`](../data/0498-creative-license-nyc-2011-2012.md).

## The argument

The 2026 severance is the only breakup in the record that was designed
before it was executed — and the design is what makes it a synthesis
rather than an event. On a base rate of 129 severance episodes with 128
resumed (100%, median gap 36 seconds, all-time maximum 46 hours), Dan
set a 120-day horizon: December 19, 2026. A test without a base rate is
a wish. This one is an experiment, with a dated falsifier, a re-check
pass scheduled to run it, and the block's current state reported as
held.

That is the argument in full: **for the first time in eleven years, an
exit from this relationship is falsifiable on a date.**

severance had no experiment around it and held; Annie's has the full
machinery and is still in progress.

## What it explains that the parts don't

No event node below this one carries the design reading, because the
design is not in the events — it is in the relationship between the
base rate and the horizon. The 120 days is meaningful *only* against
129/128 at 100% with a 46-hour maximum. Without the recount, "120 days"
is a round number expressing hope. With it, the horizon is ~62 times the
longest observed resumption gap — the statistical equivalent of
declaring that the next draw will come from a different distribution.
That is what makes this a synthesis: the experiment exists in the
space between the attachment model's number and the calendar, and
neither node contains it alone.

It also explains why the design includes its own threats to validity.
Naming the Milo channel and the Coles handle-attribution problem in
advance is not pessimism; it is the falsifier discipline applied to a
breakup. The August 26 falsifier firing within 48 hours is the design
working as designed — the boundary claim was stated in a way that let
reality contradict it, reality did, and the record kept both. A
severance that cannot be failed is not a severance; it is a story.

## Where it breaks and what it can't settle

**The outcome is in progress.** As of this writing (2026-09-09) the test
has ~100 days left. Everything in this node except the base rate is a
prediction. If the block fails in October, the node keeps its design
and loses its ending — which is exactly what the falsifiers are for.

**The base rate's denominator is the corpus, not the relationship.**
The 129 episodes are Dan-sent severance language in the 95,067-row
merged corpus through 2026-05-28. Episodes after May 28 — including the
June 1 closure itself and the August rupture — are not in the recount's
window. The base rate describes the relationship up to the point where
the experiment began, which is correct design and also a boundary on
what the number covers.

**The 52-day arithmetic is unreconciled.** June 1 + 52 days is July 23;
the fireworks email that ended the severance is dated July 4. One of
the two figures is off, and the "most durable severance" claim — the
thing the current test is trying to beat — rests partly on arithmetic
the corpus has not settled. If the true figure is 33 days, the bar is
lower than stated.

**The comparison case may not transfer.** Kristin's block held for ten
months — but Kristin was ten weeks, not eleven years, and the
attachment model was never built on her thread. That the instrument can
hold a block in the easy case says little about the hard one, and the
node does not claim otherwise.

Contemporaneous message evidence overturning a retrospective self-report, with
the loser preserved rather than deleted.

The subject, in 2025, gave a causal chain for losing two teeth that included
never having gone to a dentist. The census found a full dental episode in autumn
2017 — surgery, a graft, and a kept one-week follow-up — plus appointments in
2020 and 2024.

Three things make this worth a node beyond the fact.

**The rule was stated before it was applied**: *residue beats testimony*. A
dated message written at the time outranks a recollection given eight years
later, and the prior wiki committed to that ordering in general rather than
reaching for it here.

**The correction produced a better claim, not a smaller one.** "No maintenance"
became "maintenance is episodic and reactive" — he goes when something has
already broken, which is why the 2017 visit opens with *"a lot of work to get
done"* rather than with a cleaning. The narrowed version explains more.

**Both versions survive.** The self-report is not deleted; it is recorded as
contradicted. That matters because what a person believes about their own
history is evidence about them regardless of whether it is accurate — and here
the belief and the record diverge on something checkable, which is a more useful
object than either alone.

The prior system's best moment in anything read so far, and it costs it its own
best number.

## What survived and what did not

| | as published | re-derived, strict |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| credences, outbound | 43 | 60 |
| credences, inbound | 2 | 4 |
| **graded (not 0/50/100), outbound** | **15** | **24** |
| **graded, inbound** | **0** | **1** |

The claim the page exists to make — that he uses the probability *scale* while
everyone else uses a word — survives, and the page argues it is now worth more
for having survived a filter built to break it. The claim's rhetorical edge does
not: an infinite ratio became 22×, and "not once in a decade from 503 people"
became once.

## The half that matters more

`wiki/mind/synthesis/failure-to-launch.md` had named scoring these credences
against outcomes **"the highest-value cheap experiment named anywhere in this
cluster"**, on the reasoning that the data already existed. Reading all 24, the
correction finds it does not. They sort into claims about another person's
interior (*"98.7% sure you don't believe any of your catholicism"*),
unfalsifiable by construction; claims about unwitnessed past events (*"99% sure
it was a sedative in that"*), which nothing in the corpus resolves; and almost no
forward-looking claims about the world at all.

**Exactly one is resolvable and it resolved false.** On 2018-08-08: *"I am 75%
sure this is my last summer at Nemacolin."* `wiki/work/nemacolin-caddying`
dates the job April 2016 – November 2019 from his own resume. He worked the 2019
season.

The page's own reading is the right one and it is sharper than the experiment
would have been: *the habit is real and rare, and it is aimed almost entirely at
propositions that can never be scored. Using the scale is a genuine cognitive
signature. Using it where it could be checked is a different skill, and the
corpus contains one attempt at it.*

## Why this is recorded here

Two reasons, and neither is the finding about the subject.

**It is a self-audit that cost something.** [`dat:0023`](0023-old-wiki-measured-flag-overstated.md)
found the same shape on the intake ledger. Here the target is the page's own
headline capability claim and the recommendation of another page it feeds. A
system that files this rather than quietly restating the number is not doing the
easy version.

**It sets up the defect in
[`dat:0048`](0048-corrected-claim-still-standing-in-its-own-page.md).** The
correction is complete, dated, and in the right place — and the retracted claim
is still standing, verbatim, in a numbered list further down the same page, from
where a generated index republished it twelve days later.

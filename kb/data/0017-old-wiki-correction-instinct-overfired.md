A skepticism failure in the direction skepticism is not usually audited for.

The prior system distrusted a first-person recollection, applied a plausibility
argument against it, and was wrong. The recollection was accurate to the month.
The plausibility argument — that nine months between arrest and diversion filing
is too long — was simply an incorrect belief about how court schedules run.

The mechanism it names for its own error is the transferable part: it had been
**calibrated on a different set of captures that were reliably off by one
year**, and carried that prior into a case where it did not apply. A correction
rule learned from real errors became a source of new ones when applied outside
the conditions that produced it.

The general shape — a narrow, correct finding generalised past its evidence — is
the same failure recorded in
[`dat:0012`](0012-old-wiki-recovered-byte-exact.md), reached independently by a
different system a month earlier. Two instances is not a pattern node yet. It is
enough to make the third worth watching for.

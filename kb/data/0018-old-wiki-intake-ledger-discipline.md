The highest-quality evidence artifact in the recovered material, and the reason
is method rather than subject.

Three properties, each of which this architecture independently arrived at:

**Corrections are append-only.** Three are recorded — a misspelling that split
one substance into two headings, and two unit-selector slips entering grams as
milligrams. Each names the original value, the new value and the reason. The
page states why: *that* a correction was needed is evidence about how the
logging happens, and editing in place destroys it. That is `source` immutability,
implemented by a different system for the same reason.

**Coverage is declared, not assumed.** Quantity figures are computed only from
events carrying a number, and the count of those is printed beside every
aggregate. The page states the rule directly: "A mean over some of the events is
never cited as a mean over all of them."

**A figure was withheld because it misleads.** `bin/intake report` prints a
per-unit rate in grams per day, extrapolated from however long a unit lasted.
For a unit consumed in an evening it reports roughly double the day's actual
consumption. The page keeps it off entirely rather than printing it with a
caveat — on the reasoning that a number with a footnote still gets quoted
without one.

The last is the least common and the most transferable. Most systems that notice
a metric is misleading label it. This one deleted it.

## What the check corrects

Two compounding errors. First, the provenance: 'patron saint of liars and
fakes' is not Elliott Smith's self-description — it is the title of a Fall
Out Boy song from 2003, an era the operator was deeply inside (the export's
own 2007 timeline lists Fall Out Boy obsession alongside the phrase). The
page built an entire interpretive edifice ('Smith's exactitude about
self-deception', 'the permission to name the lie plainly') on a
misattribution. The attraction to the phrase is real — the dated 2007
traces are genuine — but the angle of attraction the page infers from a
false Smith provenance does not survive the correction. If anything the
corrected provenance (a Fall Out Boy song title used as a 2007 persona)
fits the operator's emo-era identity *better* than the page's reading.

## What the check leaves open

The 2013 Facebook-username claim itself cannot be checked directly: the
facebook-threads directory holds only MANIFEST.json and the export is not
held here. What can be said is that no dated 2013 occurrence of the phrase
exists in the export; every dated trace is 2007. If the 2013 username did
exist, it is unattested here. The operator's testimony would settle it —
but per the convention hierarchy, a contemporaneous dated record (the 2007
traces) outranks any retrospective date.

## Note on irony

The page's central claim about the phrase — that it names 'the gap
between the presented self and the thing underneath' — survives as a claim
about the operator's use of it. What failed is the claim about whose words
they were. A misattribution this clean (wrong artist, wrong year, both
verifiable) is exactly the kind of generated-metaphor failure the
veracity-ledger discipline exists to catch.

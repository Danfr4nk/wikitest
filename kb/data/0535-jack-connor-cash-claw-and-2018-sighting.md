## What the check confirms

The cash-claw anecdote, while unverifiable from the held corpus, is internally
coherent with the rest of the record: a Fayette County criminal-defence lawyer
drawing a name at a casino promotional event, in the same gambling-venue
circuit Suzanne Frank moved in, is exactly where a family would first meet a
lawyer they later retain. The page is careful to say no source states how he
was retained — that caution is carried forward, not resolved.

## What the 2018 sighting adds

The June 1, 2018 sighting — "We just saw jack Connor 😂😂😂" — is the only
post-case trace of Connor in the held corpus and the only mention of his full
name anywhere in it (a single hit). The laughter and the casual "we just saw"
read as a small-town run-in with a locally known figure, consistent with the
page's portrait. It does not show legal contact — no representation, no
payment — only that he remained a presence in the Uniontown milieu two years
after the ARD hearing. Filed at moderate confidence: the identification of
"jack Connor" as the lawyer (rather than a same-named local) rests on the
uniqueness of the name in the corpus and the Fayette County context, not on an
explicit disambiguator.

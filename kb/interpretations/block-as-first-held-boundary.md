## The thesis

The 2026-08-26 operator-observed addendum to the Annie Ulmer page reports Dan
continuing to keep Annie blocked — no reported workaround, alternate channel,
or "one last message" exception — and the page's own infobox says it *"carries
no standing facts of its own."* The finding is the behavior, not the block:
against the historical pattern (separation → contact, terminal statement →
conversation, boundary → negotiation), Dan is behaving as though the boundary
matters more than the urge to violate it
[`dat:0250`](../data/0250-annie-ulmer-2026-08-26-update-page.md).

This node argues the thesis the record now supports: **the held block is the
first observed boundary in the entire record, because every prior boundary was
violated inside the record.** The 129-episode recount gives the base rate its
final form — 129 severance declarations, 128 of 128 resumed, median gap
thirty-six seconds
[`dat:0622`](../data/0622-attachment-model-129-episodes-resolution-withdraws-110-127.md).
Against that base rate, a block still being honored weeks later is not a
stronger-than-usual boundary. It is a *different category of observation*:
the first time the behavior the boundary describes is the behavior that
happened. `ent:annie-ulmer` flags it as the first held boundary in the record
as of late August 2026.

## The record distinguishes the held block from the violated one

The complication is filed honestly in the ingest. The August 26 boundary
update's own first falsifier — *"Annie is unblocked and the old contact cycle
resumes"* — fired within 24–48 hours: Aug 27: 16 messages, Aug 28: 39, Aug
29: 108 (two-sided), Aug 30: 84, Aug 31: 65, back in the old crisis register
[`dat:0112`](../data/0112-annie-2026-08-26-boundary-update-falsifier-fired.md).
The August 26 observation was true-as-of-date and explicitly provisional; the
test ran and the boundary did not hold the week.

So the sequence is: **boundary honored Aug 26, violated Aug 27–31,
re-held after.** The "first held boundary" claim is not the Aug 26 page's
claim — that page's falsifier fired. It is the later record's claim: the
current held state per the 2026-09-09 record, after the violation and the
re-holding. The distinction matters because it is what makes the claim
falsifiable rather than hagiographic. A boundary that was never tested is a
posture; a boundary that failed once and is currently holding is an
observation with a failure already priced in.

## The Ally control

The addendum supplies its own control condition, and it is the strongest part
of the page: with Annie, boundary and no pursuit; with Ally, contact,
consistency, politeness. That pairing weakens the "emotional system gone dark"
explanation, since warmth and attachment are still being maintained elsewhere
[`dat:0250`](../data/0250-annie-ulmer-2026-08-26-update-page.md). The
boundary is selective, not global — which is exactly what makes it a boundary
rather than a shutdown. A system that had gone dark would show it everywhere;
this one shows it in exactly one channel.

## How the ingest's findings bear on it

The ingest caps the claim twice. First: block status is not directly
observable in message records — near-zero contact is consistent with a block
but also with a pause; the Aug 29–31 resumption was two-sided, so by then
contact was flowing both ways regardless of mechanism
[`dat:0112`](../data/0112-annie-2026-08-26-boundary-update-falsifier-fired.md).
Second: the 2026-08-26 addendum is single-source operator testimony about
current behavior, dated two weeks before the writing; no message corpus,
block list, or other held record independently confirms the block's state,
and the page does not claim otherwise
[`dat:0250`](../data/0250-annie-ulmer-2026-08-26-update-page.md).

This node inherits both ceilings. "First held boundary" is an attributed
observation, not a measured fact. What raises it above anecdote is the
structure around it: the falsifier that fired, the control that held, and
the 120-day horizon that will decide it (`evt:2026-12-19-severance-horizon`).

## Contradictions

- The falsifier-fired record (`dat:0112`) is the strongest evidence *against*
  the thesis and the strongest evidence *for* its honesty: the page asked to
  be tested over time, and the test ran. A thesis that survives its own
  falsifier firing once is not the same as a thesis that never faced one.
- The block-unblock loop's corrected formula — "the exit is executed; the
  deletion never is" (`dat:1292`) — predicts exactly this shape: exits
  executed, contact deleted only at the channel level, the bond un-deleted.
  The held block may be the formula working as designed, not a new capacity.

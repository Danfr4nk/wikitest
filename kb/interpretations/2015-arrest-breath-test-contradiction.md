Two first-person accounts, ten years apart, and they do not agree.

| Source | Claim |
| :--- | :--- |
| A March 2026 message, quoted by the prior wiki | *"I would have lost mine if I hadn't consented to the breath search when I got arrested."* |
| A 2026-08-02 operator capture, quoted by the prior wiki | *"I was never asked to complete field sobriety tests or take a roadside PBT."* |

Both are the same person's testimony about the same night. The prior wiki
carried the first from 2026-07-13, replaced it with the second on 2026-08-03,
and **declined to resolve the pair** — recording instead that "the most
economical reading is that the 2026 message refers to a *chemical* test
administered later at the barracks rather than to roadside testing, which would
make both statements true — but that is an inference and no source states it."

That reconciliation is plausible and it is not evidence. It is carried here as
an alternative, not as the answer.

The contradiction is preserved rather than resolved because the reconciliation
requires assuming a fact — a barracks chemical test — that nothing in either
account mentions. Adopting it would manufacture the very thing that makes both
statements survivable, which is reasoning backwards from a desire for
consistency.

**What would settle it:** the same Fayette County docket that would settle
[`evt:2015-02-possession-arrest`](../events/2015-02-possession-arrest.md). A
chemical test appears on a case record; a conversation ten years later does not.

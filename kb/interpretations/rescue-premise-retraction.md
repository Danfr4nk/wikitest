## The thesis

`wiki/mind/synthesis/the-rescue-premise.md` reads the August 18, 2026 SOS
sequence — Annie sent SOS six times, once with both dogs' names as a duress
code (*"Betty. Milo. Whatever sos words"*), Dan answered seventy-two minutes
later with *"what."* — against a latency baseline the latency page had
already retracted thirteen days earlier. The premise's reading treats the
72-minute gap as a mood-level signal against a world where Dan answers fast
and Annie is slow; the corrected tables show the highest-volume contact
answered **faster** than Dan in every measured year, 2015–2026. The premise
page is dated 2026-08-28, the latency correction 2026-08-15. The dependency is
stale by thirteen days, not by interpretation
[`dat:1296`](../data/1296-rescue-premise-stale-latency-model.md),
[`dat:1294`](../data/1294-message-latency-corrected-tables-and-stale-sections.md).

The thesis this node argues: **this is the wave-1 systemic finding wearing a
new face — corrections don't propagate because pages read pages instead of
re-deriving.** The same wave found the main Annie page correcting the June 1
exit-line attribution on 2026-08-13 while end-fight.md still carried the stale
version. Here the same failure runs at the synthesis layer: a page's
interpretive reading silently inherits a retracted measurement, and the
thirteen-day gap is the signature. It is not a reasoning error; it is a
pipeline error. The corpus is 497 pages; nobody re-checks the latency tables
when writing about a duress code.

## What the retraction history actually says

Careful about the word "retraction" in this node's own id. The rescue premise
is not a single retracted claim. Two things are true at once on the same page:

1. **The stale dependency** — the latency reading filed above
   [`dat:1296`](../data/1296-rescue-premise-stale-latency-model.md).
2. **The honest falsifier** — the page carries P5, naming the condition that
   would void it (the 72-minute SOS latency either not repeated on any
   resumption, or contact not resuming), and names the 72-minute gap as its
   unexplained item 4. The stale-model dependence and the self-falsifier
   coexist on one page.

So the page is simultaneously running a retracted instrument and publishing
the instrument's failure conditions. That combination is the prior wiki's
honest behavior — the same behavior that produced the RESOLVED blocks, the
attribution corrections, the withdrawn figures. The failure is real but so is
the machinery that caught it, thirteen days late, on the page itself.

## How the ingest's findings bear on it

`dat:1296` files the contradiction and does not resolve it: the 72-minute
response is the page's testimony about the August 18 sequence, and the
contradiction is recorded, not adjudicated. What the ingest does not do —
and what this node should not pretend to — is re-adjudicate the Aug 16–19
events, which have extensive held-verified coverage elsewhere (e.g. the
Morgantown-call leverage campaign, [`dat:0257`](../data/0257-morgantown-call-leverage-campaign.md)
via [`evt:2026-08-16`](../events/2026-08-16-annie-terminal-end.md); the
counter-ledger re-derived from the held corpus in
[`dat:0976`](../data/0976-august-counter-ledger-replicated.md)).

The August window's own adjudication — the grievance verdict, 7 supported / 2
partial / 1 false
([`dat:0975`](../data/0975-august-grievance-verdict-scores.md)) — is the
sturdier ground the rescue premise never quite stands on. The verdict grades
claims against dated rows; the premise grades a gap against a mood baseline
that turned out to be inverted. That contrast is the point: one synthesis
instrument was re-derivable from the held corpus, the other was not, and the
one that wasn't is the one that broke.

## Contradictions

- The corrected latency tables (`dat:1294`) reverse the baseline the premise
  used — but the 72-minute gap itself is not disputed by anyone. If the gap
  is the load-bearing item and the baseline is scene-setting, this node
  overstates the dependency.
- The page's own P5 is a real falsifier with a real condition. If it fires,
  the premise retracts on its own terms — which would make the premise the
  *best*-behaved synthesis page in the corpus, not the cautionary tale.

## Open questions

- Whether the 72-minute gap survives the corrected baseline as the longest
  crisis-response latency in the corpus — the check that would decide whether
  the staleness touches the core or the margin.
- Whether the rescue-premise page's 2026-08-28 dating means it was written
  before the 2026-08-15 correction had propagated to its author — a
  production-order question the export's timestamps don't settle.

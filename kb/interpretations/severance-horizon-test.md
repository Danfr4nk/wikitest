## The thesis

Dan is measuring the August 2026 Annie severance against a **120-day horizon:
December 19, 2026** (~120 days from the terminal-end window of Aug 16–19).
This is a test's design, not its outcome — and that is the point. The horizon
is the dated falsifier the wiki's own machinery demands: P4 of
the-rescue-premise states it on the-unbroken-bond page — a severance holding
past **2026-12-19** with no external agent involved is the condition under
which "the bond is unbroken" would have to be **narrowed** rather than
restated
[`dat:1209`](../data/1209-unbroken-bond-falsifier-and-recheck-machinery.md),
[`evt:2026-12-19`](../events/2026-12-19-severance-horizon.md).

This node argues: **the horizon is the best test design in the corpus,
because it is the only one with a date, a condition, and a pre-registered
consequence.** Most of the wiki's falsifiers are open-ended ("contact
resumes" — when?). The horizon names the day (2026-12-19), names the
exclusion (no external agent involved), and names what happens if the
condition is met (the thesis narrows; it does not get to restate itself).
That is a falsifier with teeth: it constrains the future behavior of the
person who wrote it, which is what separates a test from a prediction.

## The base rate it is measured against

The baseline is now quantified: **129 severance declarations, 128 of 128
resumed, median gap thirty-six seconds, all-time maximum 46 hours** — the
2026-08-23 resolution that withdrew the weaker 110-of-127 figure
[`dat:0622`](../data/0622-attachment-model-129-episodes-resolution-withdraws-110-127.md).
The dormancy-not-exit page states the rule the horizon is testing: declared
endings produce pauses, never terminations
[`dat:1384`](../data/1384-dormancy-not-exit-rule.md). So the horizon is not
testing "will contact resume" — the base rate says it will, and the June
52-day silence (`evt:2026-06-01-annie-closure`) already ran the long-silence
experiment once and broke it. The horizon is testing something narrower:
whether a severance can hold **120 days** — more than double the prior
record — without an external agent doing the holding. That is a genuinely
harder bar than anything the record has set before.

## How the ingest's findings bear on it

The ingest files the horizon as the page's own text — the December-19-2026
falsifier is the unbroken-bond page's machinery, not an independently
re-derived measurement
[`dat:1209`](../data/1209-unbroken-bond-falsifier-and-recheck-machinery.md).
The 129-episode base rate behind it is likewise relayed testimony (the
merged 95,067-row corpus is unheld). So the horizon is a well-designed test
of an honestly-measured base rate, and both the test and the base rate are
the wiki's own construction. This node does not promote either to a finding;
it argues the *design* is good — dated, conditioned, consequenced — which is
a claim about the instrument, not about the outcome.

Note the design's deliberate asymmetry: the horizon's condition is stated as
a narrowing, not a retraction. If the severance holds 120 days with no
external agent, the bond thesis doesn't die — it narrows. That is the honest
version of falsifiability for a thesis built on seventeen years of data: one
counterexample constrains the scope, it doesn't erase the archive. A
falsifier that demanded total retraction would be ignorable; this one is
expensive enough to matter and cheap enough to fire.

## Contradictions

- The June 52-day silence also set a "record," also looked
  out-of-distribution, and broke on July 23 — and the bond thesis absorbed
  it ("everything described on this page about what happened that night
  stands; what does not stand is treating it as an ending"). The machinery
  has already demonstrated it can absorb a failed long-silence test. The
  December horizon has to be different *in kind* (the no-external-agent
  condition), not just longer, or it is the same ritual with a bigger
  number.
- The "no external agent involved" condition is the load-bearing exclusion
  and the hardest to verify. Coles's presence in the record, the group-chat
  dynamics, a crisis, a legal event — the August window was saturated with
  third parties. Adjudicating "no external agent" over 120 days of a real
  life is itself an interpretive act, which puts interpretation back inside
  the test's one clean condition.

## Open questions

- Whether the horizon's December 19 date survives as the live falsifier —
  the rescue-premise page's own P5 (contact resumed inside 72 hours would
  void it) is a competing, shorter-horizon falsifier on the same thesis.
- What counts as an "external agent" if contact resumes — the adjudication
  rule for the exclusion is the part of the test design the pages leave
  thinnest.
